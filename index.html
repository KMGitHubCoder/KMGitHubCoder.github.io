Kioshi Mishiro

About Education Experience Publications Other Info.

I am currently working at the intersection of science, policy and technolgy to support responsible ocean use, as a geospatial data expert with over seven years of professional experience in marine science, geospatial information systems (GIS), and the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS). I currently serve as a GIS Officer at the International Seabed Authority (ISA), where I manage spatial database in the Area beyond the national jurisdiction, and support international decision-making, sustainable ocean governance, open data through advanced data products.
My academic background includes a Ph.D. and M.Sc. in Oceanography from Kyushu University and a B.Eng. in Aerospace Systems Engineering from Sojo University, Japan. I specialize in marine spatial data infrastructure, geospatial legal compliance, and satellite-based environmental analysis.

Experience

    GIS Officer, International Seabed Authority (ISA) (2016–Present)

    Contribution to the mission of the International Seabed Authority (ISA) through geospatial data management, legal and scientific support, environmental data analysis, and coordination with global partners. My work supports evidence-based decision-making by the Secretariat, Legal and Technical Commission (LTC), and Council, ensuring that marine data is accessible, accurate, and effectively used for sustainable seabed governance.

    Key Areas of Work

    1. Data Infrastructure and Platform Development

    • Maintain and update geospatial datasets, such as deep-sea exploration areas including contract areas, reserved areas, and Areas of Particular Environmental Interest (APEIs) in the Area beyond national jurisdiction.
    • Develop automation workflows for spatial analysis and visualization to address in the ISA context such as deep-sea exploration activities and environmental assessments.
    • Create and enhance internal/public interactive geospatial information that support discussions within the Secretariat and Commission.
    • Support ISA efforts to promote data sharing through collaboration with UN entities.
    • Provide technical guidance documents for data formatting, particularly on bathymetry and oceanographic data submissions.

    2. Legal and Technical Support

    • Provide visualized data products and geospatial analysis for ISA publications and working documents.
    • Verify sampling station data, assess contractor digital data submissions, and assist in workshop preparations.
    • Support in coordinating with external partners such as Internatioanl Hydrographic Organization (IHO) (e.g. Area2030 Initiative) in addition to the UN geospatial network.
    • Support development of ISA technology roadmap and assessment through cooperation with academic institution, in addition to organziation of the technology related workshops.

    3. Knowledge Generation and Scientific Publications

    • Contribution to ISA and external publications, including policy briefs, brochures, and technical series on deep-sea exploration and environmental management.
    • Coordination with regional bodies and individual experts, including FAO and RFMOs to integrate fisheries data in spatial planning.
    • Collaboration with scientists and consultants to promote open access and integrated analysis of environmental and exploration data.

    4. Scientific Review and Contract Oversight

    • Support the review of annual and periodic reports submitted by contractors, with a focus on environmental data and data quality.
    • Participation in reviews of Environmental Impact Assessments (EIAs) and contractor inspection preparations.
    • Summarize oceanographic and spatial findings to inform discussions and recommendations by the Commission.

    5. Intergovernmental and Strategic Collaboration

    • Engage with the UN-GGIM geospatial network and contribute to development of UN-wide geospatial strategies.
    • Coordinate with DOALOS on maritime boundary definitions and ECS/EEZ datasets relevant to the Area.
    • Facilitate technical exchanges with organizations such as ICPC to consider submarine cable data in spatial planning.

    Approach and Impact

    My approach integrates geospatial science, international cooperation, and strategic thinking to enhance ISA’s digital infrastructure, legal frameworks, and scientific outputs. By promoting data accessibility, standardization, and timely decision-support tools, I help ensure that seabed resources are governed responsibly and transparently. Collaboration, innovation, and practical solutions are central to my role within the ISA Secretariat.

    Civil Servant/Research Fellows, Mona Geoinformatics Institute (2014–2016)

    As part of a partnership between the Japan International Cooperation Agency (JICA) and the University of the West Indies (UWI) Mona campus, I contributed to coastal data collection, numerical modeling, and public outreach within the GIS research hub at Mona Geoinformatics Institute (MGI).

    Key Contributions

    • Conducted extensive fieldwork in Jamaican coastal zones to collate historical records and measurement data relevant to shoreline change and oceanographic processes.
    • Applied numerical hydrodynamic models, including the RMA Finite Element Method, to simulate coastal dynamics and analyze current and wave behavior under various environmental conditions.
    • Established and launched the Coastal Dynamics Modelling Laboratory, an accessible online platform providing the public with interactive access to numerical models and coastal data repositories.

    Impact

    This initiative enhanced public access to scientific tools and coastal data in Jamaica, strengthened local capacity in coastal modeling, and supported sustainable coastal planning and disaster resilience through informed decision-making and open data sharing.

    Civil and Oceanographic Engineer, Sanyo Techno Marine (2013–2014)

    As a Civil Engineer at Sanyo Techno Marine Inc., I conducted oceanographic monitoring and numerical modeling for environmental impact assessments, focusing on coastal and bay areas across Japan.

    Key Contributions

    • Performed field-based oceanographic monitoring through fixed-point and towed observations to assess coastal hydrodynamics, including current flow regimes and marine debris distribution.
    • Analyzed collected data to support environmental assessments, particularly in relation to coastal infrastructure and marine ecosystem impacts.
    • Applied the FVCOM (Finite-Volume Coastal Ocean Model) to simulate oceanographic conditions and evaluate the influence of thermal discharges on local marine environments.

    Impact

    Supported data-driven environmental assessments in Japanese coastal zones, strengthening the foundation for regulatory compliance and sustainable coastal development through advanced numerical modeling and field observation techniques.
